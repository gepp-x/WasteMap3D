# Hydrology data: water levels, BMA drainage inventory, Bangkok flood history, hourly event rain

Generated by `scripts/data/fetch-hydrology.ts`.

- Run all sections: `npx tsx scripts/data/fetch-hydrology.ts`
- Rebuild from `data/raw/` only: add `--offline`
- Run some sections only: `--sections=waterlevels,drainage,floods,hourly`

Raw downloads are stored under `data/raw/thaiwater/waterlevel/`, `data/raw/bma-opendata/drainage/` and `data/raw/flood-history/`. Each one has a `*.meta.json` file beside it with the URL, HTTP status and retrieval time. The script's machine-readable report, including the fetch log, counts and checks, is in `data/staged/hydrology-report.json`. Sources are listed in `data/staged/hydrology-sources.json` as SourceRef[]. Two offline reruns produce byte-identical outputs.

> **Order dependency:** `scripts/data/fetch-osm-infra.ts` rewrites `public/data/bangkok/drainage.geojson` from OSM alone. If you run it again, run `npx tsx scripts/data/fetch-hydrology.ts --sections=drainage` afterwards to re-merge the official BMA features. The merge can be run repeatedly: the script first removes any `source: "bma-dds"` features and `officialMatch` annotations, then adds them back.

## 1. Water levels: `public/data/{bangkok,hatyai}/water-levels.json` (WaterLevelStation[])

**Sources.** Two public, unauthenticated HII ThaiWater endpoints. The www.thaiwater.net front-end calls both, and they are listed in its bundle `data/raw/thaiwater/app.chunk.js.txt`. `api-v3.thaiwater.net` has no robots.txt.

| endpoint | content | HTTP | retrieved (UTC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `…/thaiwater30/public/waterlevel_load` | Latest telemetered reading per gauge across the country (809 records). Agencies: HII, RID and others. Includes station metadata: `min_bank`, `ground_level`, left/right bank, `critical_level_msl`, `situation_level`, `storage_percent`, and the API's own situation `scale`. | 200 | 2026-11-04T02:00:22Z |
| `…/thaiwater30/public/canal_waterlevel` | BMA Drainage & Sewerage Dept canal gauges (282 records). `canal_value` = level inside the canal or gate ("ระดับน้ำใน"), in m MSL ("หน่วย : (ม.รทก.)" in the front-end popup). Each record also has bank, warning and critical levels. | 200 | 2026-11-04T02:00:23Z |

**Selection.**
- Stations must have agency-published coordinates inside the given bboxes: Bangkok [100.17, 13.34, 101.09, 14.11] and Hat Yai [100.28, 6.82, 100.66, 7.21].
- A water level must be published.
- The reading must be at most 48 h older than the retrieval time. This matches the ThaiWater web map, which hides readings older than 2 days.

**Results** (snapshot of 2026-11-04 about 09:00 ICT):
- **Bangkok: 255 gauges.** 25 HII, 3 RID and 227 BMA DDS canal gauges. Readings run from 2026-11-02T13:55 to 2026-11-04T09:05 +07:00.
  - Chao Phraya gauges included: HII CPY014 Nuan Chawi bridge, HII CPY015 Krung Thep bridge, RID C.12 RID Samsen, and HII BKC003/BKC004 (Khlong Lat Bang Yo gates, Phra Pradaeng). Tha Chin, Khlong Saen Saep (AIT001 Asok), Lat Phrao, Prem Prachakorn and other canals are also covered.
  - 56 records were excluded: 55 stale BMA canal gauges (last readings from 2019 to 2026-10-28) and HII **KRP901**. KRP901 (อุทยานเฉลิมพระเกียรติ…, Dusit) publishes a water level of 29.82 with bank 30 and ground 27, all labelled m MSL. Bangkok land lies about 0–2 m MSL, so the gauge clearly uses a local datum and the value is not shown as m MSL.
- **Hat Yai: 9 gauges, all fresh.** HII ONE037, ONE038, SLA002 and SLA007; RID X.44, X.90, X.174, X.181 and X.240. **Khlong U-Taphao is covered** by X.44 (บ้านหาดใหญ่ใน), X.90, X.181, SLA002 and ONE037.

**Fields.**
- `waterLevel`: m MSL, **observed**, rounded to 0.01 m. The methodology quotes the published reading time. Timestamps are Thai local time, written as `+07:00` in `observedAt`.
- `bankLevel`, observed:
  - ThaiWater gauges: the station attribute `min_bank`. This is the bank level ThaiWater treats as 100 % in its `storage_percent`. The published left/right bank and critical levels are quoted in the methodology.
  - BMA canal gauges: the published `bank`. Values of 0 are kept as published; they occur only where the critical level is also 0.
- `capacityPct`, **derived**, ThaiWater gauges only: `(waterLevel − ground_level) / (min_bank − ground_level) × 100`. All 37 gauges with both levels (28 Bangkok + 9 Hat Yai) match ThaiWater's own `storage_percent` to within 0.21 percentage points. BMA canal gauges publish no ground (bed) level, so no percentage is derived for them. The value is the share of the bed-to-bank depth that is filled. It does **not** measure flow capacity.
- `situation` is Thai text.
  - ThaiWater gauges: the published `situation_level`, mapped to text through the API's own `scale.rule_web` and `scale.scale` tables. English for the UI:

    | Thai | English |
    |---|---|
    | น้ำล้นตลิ่ง | Overbank (>100 %) |
    | น้ำมาก | High (>70–100 %) |
    | น้ำปกติ | Normal (>30–70 %) |
    | น้ำน้อย | Low (>10–30 %) |
    | น้ำน้อยวิกฤติ | Critically low (≤10 %) |

  - BMA canal gauges: derived with the ThaiWater web-map rule from the published thresholds. Value ≥ critical gives ถึงระดับวิกฤต (at/above critical); ≥ warning gives ถึงระดับเฝ้าระวัง (at/above watch level); otherwise ต่ำกว่าระดับเฝ้าระวัง (below watch level). The same mapping is in `hydrology-report.json` under `waterLevels.situationTextEn`.
- **Ids:**
  - `<agency short name>-<published code>`, for example `rid-X.44` or `hii-CPY015`.
  - `bma-<code>` for canal gauges, for example `bma-WL.SSB.12`.
- **English names:** the API publishes no English station names. `nameEn` is built from the code and the published sub-district, district and province names.
- **Snapshot semantics:** each value states its retrieval time. Running the script again without `--offline` replaces the snapshot. Nothing is live.
- **Situation counts in this snapshot:**
  - Bangkok: 3 overbank and 16 high (river/estuary gauges, including BKC004 at 103.9 % on 2026-11-02 20:50). Of the canal gauges, 1 is at/above critical and 5 are at/above the watch level.
  - Hat Yai: 1 high (ONE037, 91.2 %) and 7 normal. X.44 reads 0.62 m MSL, 30.2 %, "normal".

## 2. Official BMA drainage inventory: `data/staged/bangkok-dds-drainage.json` and the merge into `public/data/bangkok/drainage.geojson`

**Search.** CKAN `package_search` on data.bangkok.go.th for สถานีสูบน้ำ, ประตูระบายน้ำ, อุโมงค์, pump, ระบายน้ำ and dds. The raw results are in `data/raw/bma-opendata/drainage/search_*.json`. Every licence below is "License not specified".

| dataset (publisher) | use | content |
|---|---|---|
| `water-station` (สำนักการระบายน้ำ) | **merged** | 438 rows: 201 pump stations (gp_type "บ่อสูบน้ำ") and 237 floodgates (ประตูระบายน้ำ), each with district and lat/long. CSV of 2023-05-24. |
| `pumping-stations` (สำนักการระบายน้ำ) | **merged (2022 release)** | Drainage pump sumps (บ่อสูบน้ำ) with capacity. The table holds two releases: 312 rows of 2022-04-30 in WGS84 lat/long, and 262 rows of 2020-05-25 with UTM zone 47N metres in the lat/long columns. |
| `water-drainage-tunnel` (สำนักการระบายน้ำ) | staged only | 12 tunnels with capacity, diameter, length, service area, districts and status. **No coordinates**, so no points are added to the map. |
| `pumping-station`, `floodgate` (กองยุทธศาสตร์ดิจิทัล) | cross-check | The same 201 pump stations and 237 gates (all names match). |
| `pumpstation` (2021 GIS) | cross-check, not merged | 144 older pump-station points; 84 names match the 2023 DDS inventory. Superseded. |

**Capacity column.** The water-station column `gp_gate` holds the **total pumping capacity in m³/s**.
- This is an inference. The column name is misleading, but the value equals Σ(count × unit capacity) from the published per-pump breakdown (`gp_pump`, e.g. "45 / 35(3)+10(5)") in 269 of the 272 rows that have a breakdown.
- For the 3 rows where they disagree, the capacity is withheld. 166 rows (mostly gates) publish "-", so they have no capacity.
- Units are not stated in the CSVs. They are inferred from values that match independent statements, e.g. Bang Sue tunnel 60 m³/s, Ø5 m, 6.4 km, 56 km². The 4 operational tunnels sum to **195 m³/s**, exactly the BMA figure for 2025 in `data/staged/drainage-capacity.json`.

**Mapping rules.**
- Rows without a valid coordinate (9, e.g. a phone number in the latitude column) are listed in staging and not mapped.
- The 2022 sumps are used as published.
- The 2020 sump release was converted UTM→WGS84 for comparison only. Names present in both releases lie a median 0.46 m apart (n = 212), which confirms the WGS84 datum. The 50 names found only in 2020 are listed under `olderReleases.sumps2020` and **not** merged; they may have been renamed or decommissioned.

**Merge into `drainage.geojson`.**
- All 173 OSM features are kept and now carry `source: "osm"`.
- An official feature counts as a duplicate when it has the same `kind`, a name match after removing whitespace, punctuation and generic prefixes (equal or containment, ≥3 characters), and lies ≤150 m from an OSM point. Duplicates are not added; the OSM feature instead gets `officialMatch {source, sourceId, ddsId, name, distanceM}` and `capacityM3s`/`capacitySource`. There are **7 matches**, all at 3–64 m, e.g. Phra Khanong, Krung Kasem and Pak Khlong Talat pump stations.
- **734 official points were added:** 506 `pump-station` (195 DDS pump stations + 311 sumps) and 228 `gate`.
- Properties on the added points: `{name, nameEn: null, kind, source: "bma-dds", status: "observed", sourceId, ddsId, ddsType, district?, ddsGroup?, capacityM3s?, pumpCount?, pointMethod: "agency-published-coordinate"}`.
- The file now has 907 features (411 KB). `metadata` has been updated (`statusNote`, `sourceIds`, `attribution`, `bmaDds`, `limitations`).

**Staged summary.**
- Nominal installed capacity: pump stations 1,800.11 m³/s (185 of 201 with a capacity); gates with pumps 118.72 m³/s (84); sumps 560.52 m³/s (312).
- Tunnels by status:

  | status | tunnels | capacity | length |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | operational | 4 | 195 m³/s | 19.37 km |
  | under construction | 4 | 182 m³/s | 28.73 km |
  | awaiting budget | 3 | 122 m³/s | 14.6 km |
  | feasibility study | 1 | 60 m³/s | 6 km |

- Per-district counts and capacity are in `byDistrict`, with district names as published (one typo, "เขคภาษีเจริญ", is kept verbatim).
- **These are sums of nominal installed capacities, not a simultaneous or effective citywide capacity.** By comparison, the BMA states 1,300 m³/s of pumping to the Chao Phraya and ran pumps at "1,200 m³/s" in September 2026.

## 3. Bangkok flood history: `data/staged/bangkok-flood-events.json` (FloodEvent[] plus extra fields)

Three events. Every quoted number is checked by the script against the cached source text (whitespace collapsed): **16/16 quotes found verbatim**.

| id | from → to | peakRainMm | main source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bkk-flood-2011` | 2011-10-01 → 2011-11-30 | *(none: the flood was driven by river water and runoff; rain and tide values are in `peakValues`)* | HII "บันทึกเหตุการณ์มหาอุทกภัยปี 2554" (tiwrmdev.hii.or.th/current/menu.html) + World Bank 2012 overview |
| `bkk-flood-2022-05-17` | 2022-05-17 → 2022-05-18 | 136.0 mm/day, observed (ซ.สะพานขวา, BMA DDS gauge, 07:00 17 May → 07:00 18 May) | ThaiWater/HII event summary FloodBKK_May2022 |
| `bkk-flood-2026-09-25` | 2026-09-24 → 2026-09-27 | 210.0 mm/day, observed (เทศบาลสงเคราะห์, Chatuchak, 07:00 25 Sep → 07:00 26 Sep) | BMA figures reported by Thai PBS (558661, 558595, 558588) |

**2011 facts, all quoted.**
- Bangkok's worst flood in 70 years (since 1942).
- The DDPM October 2011 report, as reproduced by HII, lists Bangkok with **32 districts (เขต)** affected.
- Bangkok rainfall from 1 Jan to 1 Dec 2011: **2,257.5 mm**. October 2011: **388.0 mm**. Both from BMA DDS gauges.
- Tide peaks at the Royal Thai Navy HQ gauge: **2.53 m on 30 Oct** and **2.41 m on 12 Nov 2011**. The datum is not stated in the source and is recorded as such in the unit.
- National figures (World Bank): THB 1.43 trillion (USD 46.5 billion) in damage and losses; more than 13 million people affected from July through December 2011.
- HII names Sai Mai and Khlong Sam Wa as areas that could not drain. No complete per-district list is published in the cited sources, so none is invented: `affectedDistricts.namedTh` contains only those two names.
- `from`/`to` bound the window in which DDPM reports list Bangkok as affected. They are not district-level onset or recession dates (see `eventWindowNote`).
- HII's text gives two different TMD 30-year normals for end of November (1,973.5 and 1,641.9 mm). Neither is used.

**May 2022.** 35 BMA gauges exceeded 90 mm/day, 21 of them over 100 mm. The source's English text gives the window as 17 May 07:00 → 19 May 07:00, but the Thai original says one day (→ 18 May 07:00); the Thai is used.

**September 2026.**
- Almost 48 h of rain from 16:00 on 24 Sep 2026, close to 300 mm in total (Min Buri 274.5 mm and Khlong Sam Wa 273.0 mm by 06:00 on 26 Sep).
- The BMA disaster-area declaration was extended from 3 districts (Nong Chok, Suan Luang, Khan Na Yao) to **all 50**, effective 25 Sep 2026. This is a legal designation, not a measured flood extent.
- Pumps ran at a combined 1,200 m³/s.
- The source is a broadcaster reporting BMA statements. The BMA's own report was not retrieved, and the provenance says so.

**Extra fields.** These are not in the FloodEvent contract; the zod schema is non-strict, so they pass:
- `sourceIds[]`
- `peakValues[]`, each `{label, value, unit, period, place, status: "observed", sourceId, quoteTh | quoteEn}`
- `affectedDistricts {count, namedTh[], scope, sourceId}` or null
- `eventWindowNote`

The integrator can copy the array as-is to `public/data/bangkok/flood-events.json`. Its zod validation passes (checked with `FloodEventSchema`). The new `sourceId`s must be added to `public/data/sources.json` from `hydrology-sources.json`.

**Failed or not used.**
- The full World Bank report on gfdrr.org returned **HTTP 403** (bot protection). It was not bypassed; the overview edition from the Open Development Mekong mirror was used instead.
- Guessed ThaiWater event URLs (FloodBKK_Sep2022, …) return a soft-404 home page and were not used.
- Wikipedia, news timelines and blogs were not used as sources.

## 4. Hourly rain: `public/data/{hatyai,bangkok}/rain-hourly.json` (HourlyRainEvent[], status **estimated**)

These are converted one-to-one from `data/staged/rain-hourly-events.json`: ERA5 reanalysis from Open-Meteo, `models=era5`, source `open-meteo-archive-era5`, registered in `rain-sources.json`.

- **Hat Yai**: `hatyai-2025-11-18_2025-11-27`, "ฝนรายชั่วโมง เหตุการณ์อุทกภัยหาดใหญ่ 18–27 พ.ย. 2568 (ข้อมูลประมาณการ ERA5)".
  - 240 hours, total 811 mm, peak hour 21.7 mm (ending 21 Nov 12:00).
  - Gauge comparison: ThaiWater reports 370.2 mm/day at TMD Hat Yai on 21 Nov, against 240 mm for the ERA5 cell's local day.
- **Bangkok**: `bangkok-heaviest-day-2026-09-26`, "ฝนรายชั่วโมง ช่วงฝนตกหนักกรุงเทพฯ 25–27 ก.ย. 2569 (ข้อมูลประมาณการ ERA5)".
  - 72 hours, total 197.1 mm.
  - The window coincides with the documented September 2026 flood. Gauge comparison: BMA gauges recorded 210.0 mm in 24 h, against 88 mm for the ERA5 cell's wettest local day.

**How the files were built.**
- `lon`/`lat` is the requested reference point. The answering 0.25° ERA5 cell is named in the provenance.
- Times are local (+07:00). Each value is rain in the preceding hour.
- Checks: Σ hourly = staged event total, lengths match, no nulls.
- Each provenance repeats the methodology and limitations, plus a gauge-comparison limitation: these series are for timing and shape, **not depth**.

## 6. Caveats

- Water levels are a one-time snapshot, are not quality-controlled, and are not live.
- Datums: values are as published in m MSL (ม.รทก.). One gauge on an evidently local datum is excluded. Others may have survey errors that cannot be detected from the API.
- The meaning of the BMA DDS `gp_gate` column is inferred; it is verified against the pump breakdown in 269 of 272 rows.
- The BMA inventory is a dated release (2022/2023). Structures built since then are missing. Capacities are nominal.
- Flood-history numbers are exactly as published. Translations are this project's own. The September 2026 figures are reported by media, not taken from a BMA primary document.
